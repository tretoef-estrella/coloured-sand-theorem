\section*{Abstract}

Let $H(d,q)=K_q^{\,d}$ be the Hamming graph and $K(H(d,q))$ its sandpile group (critical group, Jacobian). For primes $p\nmid q$ its $p$-part was determined by Jacobson, Niedermaier and Reiner in 2003, who wrote that the other primes appear ``to be difficult, due to bad behavior of its $p$-primary structure''. {\boldmath\textbf{We determine the Sylow $p$-subgroup of $K(H(d,q))$ for every prime $p$ dividing $q$ and every $d$, by an explicit rule.}} After the splitting of Jacobson, Niedermaier and Reiner, the group is a sum of cokernels of operators $q(c+H)+U$ on the Boolean lattice (Theorem~A). These split over the indecomposable tilting modules $T(\lambda)$ of $SL_2$ in characteristic $p$ that occur in a tensor power of the natural module. The contribution of $T(\lambda)$ consists of explicit small cyclic factors and of $2^{|I|}$ large ones, where $I$ is the set of positions of the nonzero base-$p$ digits of $\lambda+1$ below the leading one. The large ones are obtained by pooling explicit integers, built from binomial valuations and base-$p$ carries, into a non-increasing sequence (Theorems~I and~J). The proof writes down explicit $\Z_p$-lattices for all tilting modules of $SL_2$, one move per base-$p$ digit; it reduces each contribution to the Smith form of an explicit $2^{|I|}\times2^{|I|}$ matrix, and computes that form as in our proof for the hypercube \cite{DST}, the case $p=2$. We also give closed formulas that need no pooling: for $p>d$ with ballot numbers (B), for the exponent with every prime (C), and for the number of cyclic factors (D). This proves Conjecture~I of version~3. {\boldmath\textbf{Every result of the paper, Theorems A--J, is checked in Lean~4 with Mathlib, for every prime, and the Lean project was rebuilt from its sources on GitHub's machines, on Mathlib's published build (\S\ref{sec:lean}).}} This work has not been refereed by a human expert.

\section{Introduction}

For a connected graph $\Gamma$ with Laplacian $L(\Gamma)$, the sandpile group $K(\Gamma)$ is the torsion subgroup of $\coker L(\Gamma)$, and $\coker L(\Gamma)\cong\Z\oplus K(\Gamma)$; its order is the number of spanning trees \cite{Big}. The Hamming graph $H(d,q)$ has vertex set $(\Z/q)^d$, two vertices being adjacent when they differ in exactly one coordinate; it is the Cartesian power $K_q^{\,d}$ of the complete graph. Its Laplacian eigenvalues are $qs$, $0\le s\le d$, with multiplicity $\binom ds(q-1)^s$.

Jacobson, Niedermaier and Reiner \cite{JNR} determined the Sylow $p$-subgroup of $K(K_{n_1}\times\cdots\times K_{n_k})$ for every prime $p$ that divides none of the $n_i$ \cite[Theorem~2]{JNR}, and bounded the number of invariant factors \cite[Theorem~3]{JNR}. About the other primes they wrote: ``Describing the exact structure of the critical group $K(K_{n_1}\times\cdots\times K_{n_k})$ appears to be difficult, due to bad behavior of its $p$-primary structure for primes $p$ that divide some of $n_1,\dots,n_k$.'' Ducey and Jalil \cite{DJ} recovered the results of \cite{JNR} by another method. For $d=2$ the whole group $K(H(2,q))=K(K_q\times K_q)$ was determined by Ducey, Gerhard and Watson \cite[Theorem~1.1]{DGW}; it also follows from \cite[Theorem~13 and Example~11]{JNR}. Our results agree with it at $d=2$, and are new, to the best of our knowledge, for $d\ge3$. For $q=2$ the graph is the hypercube; its odd part is due to Bai \cite{Bai} and its $2$-part was determined in \cite{DST}.

In this paper $q\ge2$ is arbitrary, $p$ is a prime dividing $q$, and $e:=v_p(q)\ge1$, except where we say otherwise.

\subsection{The pieces}
Let $B_m$ be the free abelian group on the subsets of $[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}$, graded by size: $B_m=\bigoplus_{s=0}^m B_m(s)$. Let $H$ act on $B_m(s)$ as the scalar $s$, and let $U$ and $D$ be the up and down operators,
\[
U(S)=\sum_{x\notin S}S\cup\{x\},\qquad D(S)=\sum_{x\in S}S\setminus\{x\}.
\]
For integers $c\ge0$ put
\[
X_q(m,c):=\coker\big(q(c+H)+U\ \text{ on } B_m\big).
\]
It is finite when $c\ge1$; for $c=0$ it is $\Z$ plus a finite group.

\begin{theorem}[Jacobson--Niedermaier--Reiner {\cite[Theorem~13]{JNR}}]\label{thA}
For every $q\ge2$ and $d\ge1$, as abelian groups,
\[
\coker L\big(H(d,q)\big)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{j=0}^{d} X_q(d-j,\,j)^{\oplus\binom dj (q-2)^j}.
\]
\end{theorem}

This is the case $n_1=\dots=n_d=q$ of \cite[Theorem~13]{JNR}: there the pieces are upper triangular matrices $M(N;n_S)$ of size $2^{|S|}$ with identity blocks above the diagonal, and after reversing the order of the subsets of $S$ they are the matrices $q(c+H)+U$. We include the short proof below, in the form we use. For $q=2$ only $j=0$ survives and the theorem says $\coker L(Q_d)\cong X_2(d,0)$, the starting point of \cite{DST}.

\begin{theorem}[the primes $p>d$]\label{thB}
Let $d\ge1$, let $p>d$ be a prime dividing $q$, and $e=v_p(q)$. Then
\[
\Syl_p K\big(H(d,q)\big)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{v=1}^{d}\big(\Z/p^{ev}\big)^{n_v},\qquad
n_v=\sum_{\substack{j+2k=d+1-v\\ (j,k)\neq(0,0)}}\binom{d}{j}\,(q-2)^{j}\,b(k,v),
\]
the sum over integers $j,k\ge0$ (the condition $(j,k)\ne(0,0)$ is automatic, since $j+2k=d+1-v\ge1$), where $b(k,v)=\frac{v}{v+2k}\binom{v+2k}{k}=[x^k]\,C(x)^v$ are the ballot numbers and $C(x)=\sum_n\frac1{n+1}\binom{2n}nx^n$ is the Catalan series.
\end{theorem}

Equivalently, with $c(z)=C(z^2)$ and $u=q-2$, $\sum_v n_v y^v=[z^{d+1}]\,(1+uz)^d\,\frac{yz\,c(z)}{1-yz\,c(z)}-y^{d+1}$. For instance, with $u=q-2$:
\begin{align*}
d=2:&\quad (\Z/p^{e})^{u^2+1}\oplus(\Z/p^{2e})^{2u},\\
d=3:&\quad (\Z/p^{e})^{u^3+3u}\oplus(\Z/p^{2e})^{3u^2+2}\oplus(\Z/p^{3e})^{3u},\\
d=4:&\quad (\Z/p^{e})^{u^4+6u^2+2}\oplus(\Z/p^{2e})^{4u^3+8u}\oplus(\Z/p^{3e})^{6u^2+3}\oplus(\Z/p^{4e})^{4u}.
\end{align*}
Theorem~\ref{thB} is the case $m<p$ of a statement about single pieces (Theorem~\ref{thBprime} in Section~3), which also gives, for every prime $p$, the contribution of all pieces with $d-j<p$.

\begin{theorem}[the exponent]\label{thC}
Let $q\ge3$, let $p$ be any prime and $e=v_p(q)$. The exponent of $\Syl_p K(H(d,q))$ is $p^{E}$, where
\[
E=\max_{1\le s\le d}\big(e\,s+v_p(s)\big)\quad\text{if } e\ge1,\qquad E=\lfloor\log_p d\rfloor\quad\text{if } e=0 .
\]
\end{theorem}

For $p>d$ this gives $E=ed$, as in Theorem~\ref{thB}. For $p\le d$ the maximum need not be at $s=d$: for $q=3$ and $d=10$ it is at $s=9$ and $E=11$. The case $e=0$ ($p\nmid q$) can also be read off from \cite[Theorem~2]{JNR}; we include it because the proof below covers it at no cost.

\begin{theorem}[the number of cyclic factors]\label{thD}
Let $p\mid q$. The number of cyclic factors of $\Syl_p K(H(d,q))$ is
\[
N=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom dj(q-2)^j\Big(2^{\,d-j}-\rho_p(d-j)\Big)\;-\;1,\qquad \rho_p(m)=\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}r_p(m,s),
\]
where $r_p(m,s)=\sum_{i}\big(\binom mi-\binom m{i-1}\big)$, the sum over $0\le i\le t$ with $p\nmid k-i$, and $(t,k)=(s,s+1)$ if $2s\le m-1$, $(t,k)=(m-1-s,m-s)$ otherwise.
\end{theorem}

Here $\rho_p(m)$ is the rank over $\F_p$ of $U$ on $B_m$. For $q=p=3$ the theorem gives $N=1,4,12,34,96,274,\dots$, and $N=3^{d-1}+2^{d-1}-1$ for every $d$ (Corollary~\ref{corN}). For $q=p$ the value of $N$ equals the bound $\frac12(q^d+(q-2)^d)-1$ of \cite[Theorem~3]{JNR} for $d\le2$, is one less for $d=3$, and is strictly smaller for $4\le d\le8$ and $p\le11$ (where we compared).

\begin{corollary}\label{corq}
$\Syl_p K(H(d,q))$ depends on $q$ only through $e=v_p(q)$ and the multiplicities $\binom dj(q-2)^j$.
\end{corollary}

\section{The splitting (after Jacobson, Niedermaier and Reiner)}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thA}]
Let $K_q=qI-J$ be the Laplacian of the complete graph $K_q$, acting on $\Z^q$ with basis $e_0,\dots,e_{q-1}$, and put $f_i=e_i-e_0$ ($1\le i\le q-1$) and $\sigma=f_1+\cdots+f_{q-1}$. Then $e_0,\sigma,f_1,\dots,f_{q-2}$ is a basis of $\Z^q$ (unimodular change from $e_0,f_1,\dots,f_{q-1}$), and
\[
K_qe_0=-\sigma,\qquad K_q\sigma=q\sigma,\qquad K_qf_i=qf_i .
\]
So $\Z^q=A\oplus W$ with $A=\Z e_0\oplus\Z\sigma$ and $W=\bigoplus_{i\le q-2}\Z f_i$, both stable under $K_q$, and $K_q$ is the scalar $q$ on $W$. The Laplacian of $H(d,q)$ is $L=\sum_{i=1}^d 1\otimes\cdots\otimes K_q\otimes\cdots\otimes1$ on $(\Z^q)^{\otimes d}=\bigoplus_{T\subseteq[d]}A^{\otimes([d]\setminus T)}\otimes W^{\otimes T}$, and each summand is $L$-stable. On the summand of $T$ with $|T|=j$, $L$ acts as $(L_A+qj)\otimes1$, where $L_A=\sum_i K_q^{(i)}$ on $A^{\otimes(d-j)}$ and $W^{\otimes T}\cong\Z^{(q-2)^j}$. There are $\binom dj$ sets $T$ of size $j$.

Index the basis of $A^{\otimes m}$ by the subsets $S\subseteq[m]$, the factor in position $i$ being $\sigma$ if $i\in S$ and $e_0$ otherwise. Then $L_A(S)=q|S|\,S-\sum_{x\notin S}S\cup\{x\}$, that is, $L_A=qH-U$, and $L_A+qj=q(j+H)-U$. Conjugating by the diagonal matrix $(-1)^{|S|}$ turns $-U$ into $U$. Hence the summand contributes $\coker(q(j+H)+U\text{ on }B_{d-j})=X_q(d-j,j)$, $\binom dj(q-2)^j$ times.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{corq}]
By Theorem~\ref{thA} it suffices to see that $\Syl_p X_q(m,c)$ depends only on the $p$-adic valuations of the diagonal entries $a_s=q(c+s)$. This is Lemma~\ref{profile}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[only the valuations matter]\label{profile}
Let $a_0,\dots,a_m$ be elements of $\Z_{(p)}$ and $M=\operatorname{diag}(a_{|S|})+U$ on $B_m\otimes\Z_{(p)}$. If $a_s=p^{w_s}u_s$ with units $u_s$ (or $a_s=0$, with $u_s=1$), then $M$ is equivalent over $\Z_{(p)}$ to
\[\operatorname{diag}(p^{w_{|S|}})+U\qquad(\text{with } p^{w}:=0 \text{ when } a_s=0).\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Look for units $\lambda_s,\mu_s$ such that $P=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{|S|})$ and $Q=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_{|S|})$ give $PMQ=\operatorname{diag}(p^{w_{|S|}})+U$. The diagonal of $PMQ$ on rank $s$ is $\lambda_s a_s\mu_s$ and its block from rank $s$ to rank $s+1$ is $\lambda_{s+1}\mu_s U$, so we need $\lambda_s\mu_su_s=1$ and $\lambda_{s+1}\mu_s=1$. Put $\mu_0=1$, $\lambda_0=u_0^{-1}$, and recursively $\lambda_{s+1}=\mu_s^{-1}$, $\mu_{s+1}=(\lambda_{s+1}u_{s+1})^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Strings, and the primes $p>m$}

On $B_m$ one has $[D,U]=m-2H$: on a set $S$ of size $s$, $DU(S)=(m-s)S+\Sigma$ and $UD(S)=s\,S+\Sigma$ with the same sum $\Sigma=\sum_{y\in S,\,x\notin S}(S\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}$. For $0\le k\le m/2$ let $\Lambda_k=\ker D\cap B_m(k)$. By induction on $i$, for $v\in\Lambda_k$,
\begin{equation}\label{DU}
D\,U^iv=i\,(m-2k-i+1)\,U^{i-1}v .
\end{equation}
Over $\Q$ this is the classical $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-structure of the Boolean lattice \cite{Pro}:
\[B_m\otimes\Q=\bigoplus_{k\le m/2}\ \bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-2k}U^i\Lambda_k^{\Q},\]
$\dim\Lambda^{\Q}_k=\binom mk-\binom m{k-1}$, and $U^{m-2k+1}\Lambda_k=0$. We call $\Lambda_k,U\Lambda_k,\dots,U^{m-2k}\Lambda_k$ a \emph{string}; its ranks are $k,\dots,m-k$ and its length is $m-2k+1$.

\begin{lemma}[strings over $\Z_{(p)}$]\label{strings}
If $p>m$, then $B_m\otimes\Z_{(p)}=\bigoplus_{k\le m/2}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-2k}U^i\Lambda_k$, each $\Lambda_k$ is free of rank $\binom mk-\binom m{k-1}$, and $U^i\colon\Lambda_k\to U^i\Lambda_k$ is an isomorphism for $i\le m-2k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All lattices below are over $R=\Z_{(p)}$. First, for $r\le m/2$ we show $B_m(r)=U B_m(r-1)\oplus\Lambda_r$, by induction on $r$ (for $r=0$, $\Lambda_0=B_m(0)$). By induction $B_m(r-1)=\bigoplus_{k<r}U^{r-1-k}\Lambda_k$, and by \eqref{DU} the operator $DU$ acts on $U^{r-1-k}\Lambda_k$ as the scalar $(r-k)(m-k-r+1)$; both factors lie in $[1,m]$, so they are units. Hence $DU$ is invertible on $B_m(r-1)$, $\pi=U(DU)^{-1}D$ is an idempotent on $B_m(r)$ with image $UB_m(r-1)$, and its kernel is $\ker D\cap B_m(r)=\Lambda_r$ because $U(DU)^{-1}$ is injective. This gives the decomposition, and $\Lambda_r$ is a direct summand, so free, of rank $\binom mr-\binom m{r-1}$.

Now fix any rank $r$ and consider $\Phi\colon\bigoplus_{k\le\min(r,m-r)}\Lambda_k\to B_m(r)$, $(v_k)\mapsto\sum_kU^{r-k}v_k$. For $k'\le r$ let $\psi_{k'}\colon B_m(r)\to\Lambda_{k'}$ be $D^{r-k'}$ followed by the projection of $B_m(k')=UB_m(k'-1)\oplus\Lambda_{k'}$ onto $\Lambda_{k'}$. By \eqref{DU}, $D^{r-k'}U^{r-k}v_k$ is $0$ if $k>k'$ (since $Dv_k=0$), lies in $U^{k'-k}\Lambda_k\subseteq UB_m(k'-1)$ if $k<k'$, and equals $\prod_{i=1}^{r-k}i(m-2k-i+1)\,v_k$ if $k=k'$, a unit multiple of $v_k$ (all factors lie in $[1,m]$ because $k\le m-r$). So $(\psi_{k'})\circ\Phi$ is triangular with unit diagonal, $\Phi$ is split injective, and its image is a direct summand of $B_m(r)$ of full rank (compare ranks over $\Q$). Hence $\Phi$ is an isomorphism. Finally $U^{m-2k+1}\Lambda_k=0$ holds over $\Q$, hence over $R$.
\end{proof}

Each string is stable under $q(c+H)+U$: in the basis $v,Uv,\dots,U^{\ell-1}v$ ($\ell=m-2k+1$) the operator is lower bidiagonal with diagonal entries $q(c+k+i)$, $0\le i<\ell$, and all subdiagonal entries $1$. The cokernel of such a matrix over a principal ideal domain is cyclic of order its determinant, because the minor obtained by deleting the first row and last column equals $1$; if one diagonal entry is $0$ (only possible for $c=k=i=0$) the cokernel is $\Z_{(p)}$. This proves:

\begin{theorem}[pieces with $m<p$]\label{thBprime}
Let $p$ be any prime, $e=v_p(q)$, and $m<p$. Then
\[
\Syl_p X_q(m,c)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\substack{0\le k\le m/2\\(c,k)\ne(0,0)}}\big(\Z/p^{A_k}\big)^{\binom mk-\binom m{k-1}},\qquad A_k=\sum_{r=k}^{m-k}\big(e+v_p(c+r)\big),
\]
and for $c=0$ the string $k=0$ contributes only the free summand $\Z$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thB}]
If $p>d$, every piece $X_q(d-j,j)$ has $m=d-j<p$ and $c+r\le d<p$, so $v_p(c+r)=0$ for $c+r\ge1$ and $A_k=e(m-2k+1)$. Put $v=m-2k+1=d+1-j-2k$; then $\binom mk-\binom m{k-1}=\binom{v+2k-1}{k}-\binom{v+2k-1}{k-1}=\frac{v}{v+2k}\binom{v+2k}k=b(k,v)$. Summing over $j$ with Theorem~\ref{thA} gives the formula. The generating function follows from $\sum_kb(k,v)x^k=C(x)^v$.
\end{proof}

\section{The exponent}

\begin{lemma}\label{inverse}
Let $c\ge1$ and $M=q(c+H)+U$ on $B_m$. Then $M$ is invertible over $\Q$, and for $S\subseteq T$ with $|S|=s$, $|T|=s+k$,
\[
(M^{-1})_{T,S}=(-1)^k\,k!\,\prod_{i=0}^{k}\frac1{q(c+s+i)},
\]
while $(M^{-1})_{T,S}=0$ if $S\not\subseteq T$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With $\Delta=q(c+H)$, $M^{-1}=(1+\Delta^{-1}U)^{-1}\Delta^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}(-\Delta^{-1}U)^k\Delta^{-1}$, a finite sum. A path from $S$ to $T$ in $(\Delta^{-1}U)^k\Delta^{-1}$ is a maximal chain $S=S_0\subset\cdots\subset S_k=T$, there are $k!$ of them, and each collects $\Delta^{-1}$ once at every rank from $s$ to $s+k$.
\end{proof}

For a nonsingular square matrix over a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $p$, the exponent of the cokernel is $p^{\epsilon}$ with $-\epsilon$ the least valuation of an entry of $M^{-1}$ (write $M$ in Smith form). Hence, for $c\ge1$, the exponent of $\Syl_pX_q(m,c)$ is $p$ to the power
\[
\max_{\substack{s,k\ge0\\ s+k\le m}}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\big(e+v_p(c+s+i)\big)-v_p(k!)\Big)
=\max_{c\le a\le b\le c+m}\Big(eL+v_p(L)+v_p\tbinom bL\Big),\quad L=b-a+1,
\]
where $a=c+s$, $b=c+s+k$, and we used $\frac{b!}{(a-1)!\,(b-a)!}=L\binom bL$.

\begin{lemma}[carries]\label{carries}
For $d\ge1$ and $e\ge1$,
\[
\max_{1\le L\le b\le d}\Big(eL+v_p(L)+v_p\tbinom bL\Big)=\max_{1\le s\le d}\big(es+v_p(s)\big),
\]
and for $e=0$ the left side equals $\lfloor\log_p d\rfloor$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Taking $L=b=s$ gives $\ge$. Let $\lambda=\lfloor\log_pb\rfloor$. By Kummer's theorem $v_p\binom bL$ is the number of carries in the addition $L+(b-L)$ in base $p$. No carry occurs at a position below $v_p(L)$, where the digits of $L$ vanish, and a carry out of position $i$ forces $b\ge p^{i+1}$, so $i\le\lambda-1$. Hence $v_p(L)+v_p\binom bL\le\lambda$. For $e=0$ this gives the bound $\lfloor\log_pd\rfloor$, attained at $L=b=p^{\lfloor\log_pd\rfloor}$.

Let $e\ge1$; we induct on $b$, the case $b=1$ being clear. Let $s^*=p^\lambda\lfloor b/p^\lambda\rfloor$, so $1\le s^*\le b<s^*+p^\lambda$ and $v_p(s^*)\ge\lambda$. If $L\le s^*$, then $eL+v_p(L)+v_p\binom bL\le es^*+\lambda\le es^*+v_p(s^*)$. If $L>s^*$, write $b=s^*+r$ and $L=s^*+r'$ with $1\le r'\le r<p^\lambda$. Then $v_p(L)=v_p(r')$, and since $(b-L)+r'=r<p^\lambda$, the additions $L+(b-L)$ and $r'+(r-r')$ have the same carries, so $v_p\binom bL=v_p\binom r{r'}$. By induction there is $1\le t\le r$ with $er'+v_p(r')+v_p\binom r{r'}\le et+v_p(t)$. Since $t<p^\lambda\le p^{v_p(s^*)}$, $v_p(s^*+t)=v_p(t)$, and so $eL+v_p(L)+v_p\binom bL\le e(s^*+t)+v_p(s^*+t)$ with $s^*+t\le b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thC}]
By Theorem~\ref{thA} the exponent is the largest exponent of the pieces $\Syl_pX_q(d-j,j)$ that occur. For $j\ge1$ the piece occurs ($q\ge3$) and the computation above gives the maximum over $j\le a\le b\le d$; for $j=1$ this is the full range $1\le a\le b\le d$. For $j=0$, the row of the empty set in $M_0=qH+U$ is zero and $M_0$ maps into $B'=\bigoplus_{s\ge1}B_d(s)$, so
\[\coker M_0=\Z\oplus B'/(M''B'+\Z u),\]
where $M''$ is the restriction of $M_0$ to $B'$ (nonsingular, lower triangular by rank with diagonal $qs$) and $u=M_0(\emptyset)$. Its torsion is a quotient of $\coker M''$, whose exponent is given by Lemma~\ref{inverse} (with $c=0$ and $s\ge1$) as the same maximum over $1\le a\le b\le d$. So the exponent is $p$ to $\max_{1\le a\le b\le d}(eL+v_p(L)+v_p\binom bL)$, and Lemma~\ref{carries} finishes.
\end{proof}

For $q=2$ the same argument gives only an upper bound, and it is not attained: for $Q_2$ the group is $\Z/4$ while the bound is $2^3$.

\section{The number of cyclic factors}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thD}]
The number of cyclic factors of $\Syl_p K$ is $\dim_{\F_p}(\coker L\otimes\F_p)-1$, the $1$ for the free summand. Modulo $p$, $q(j+H)+U\equiv U$, so by Theorem~\ref{thA} $\dim_{\F_p}(\coker L\otimes\F_p)=\sum_j\binom dj(q-2)^j\big(2^{d-j}-\rank_{\F_p}U|_{B_{d-j}}\big)$. The rank of $U$ is the sum over $s$ of the ranks of the inclusion matrices $W_{s,s+1}$ of $s$-subsets versus $(s+1)$-subsets of $[m]$. By Wilson's diagonal form \cite[Theorem~2]{Wil}, for $t\le\min(k,m-k)$ the matrix $W_{t,k}$ is equivalent over $\Z$ to a diagonal matrix with entries $\binom{k-i}{t-i}$ of multiplicity $\binom mi-\binom m{i-1}$, $0\le i\le t$ (the summary in \cite[(2)]{CSX} states only the range $t\le k\le m/2$, which misses the middle rank $2s=m-1$ of odd $m$; Wilson's range covers it); so its rank over $\F_p$ is $\sum_{i:\,p\nmid\binom{k-i}{t-i}}(\binom mi-\binom m{i-1})$. For $W_{s,s+1}$ with $2s\le m-1$ take $(t,k)=(s,s+1)$, so $\binom{k-i}{t-i}=s+1-i$; otherwise complementation identifies $W_{s,s+1}$ with the transpose of $W_{m-s-1,m-s}$ and we take $(t,k)=(m-1-s,m-s)$. This is $r_p(m,s)$.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[the number of factors when $3\mid q$]\label{corN}
If $3\mid q$, the number of cyclic factors of $\Syl_3K(H(d,q))$ is
\[
N=(q-1)^d+\frac{q^d-(q-1)^d}{3}-\frac{(q-1)^d-(q-3)^d}{6}-1 .
\]
In particular $N=3^{d-1}+2^{d-1}-1$ for $q=3$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thD} it suffices to know $f(m)=\dim_{\F_3}\coker(U|_{B_m\otimes\F_3})$. Identify $B_m\otimes\F_3$ with $V^{\otimes m}$, $V=\F_3^2$, so that $U=\sum_ie_i$ with $e$ the nilpotent $2\times2$ Jordan block acting on the $i$-th factor. The $e_i$ commute, so $U^3=\sum_ie_i^3=0$, and $V^{\otimes m}$ is a module over the Hopf algebra $\F_3[e]/(e^3)$ with $e$ primitive. Then $f(m)$ is the number of Jordan blocks of $U$. Write $J_a$ for the indecomposable module of dimension $a\le3$. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $J_1\otimes J_2=J_2$;
\item $J_2\otimes J_2=J_3\oplus J_1$, since $e^2(v_0\otimes v_0)=2\,v_1\otimes v_1\ne0$;
\item $J_3\otimes J_2=J_3^2$, because $J_3$ is free, a projective tensored with any module is projective, and projectives over this local algebra are free.
\end{itemize}
So $V^{\otimes m}=a_mJ_1\oplus b_mJ_2\oplus c_mJ_3$ with $(a_{m+1},b_{m+1},c_{m+1})=(b_m,a_m,b_m+2c_m)$, starting from $(1,0,0)$. Hence $a_m+b_m=1$, $b_m=[m\text{ odd}]$, and counting dimensions, $c_m=(2^m-1-[m\text{ odd}])/3$. This gives $f(m)=1+(2^m-1-[m\text{ odd}])/3$. Summing $\sum_m\binom dm(q-2)^{d-m}f(m)$ gives the formula.
\end{proof}

\section{The range $p\le d$: tilting lattices}

By Theorem~\ref{thBprime} the only pieces not yet described are $X_q(m,c)$ with $m\ge p$. For $p\le m$ the strings of length $>p$ break: by \eqref{DU} the coefficient $i(m-2k-i+1)$ vanishes modulo $p$. The way they break is governed by the representation theory of $SL_2$ in characteristic $p$.

\subsection{The dictionary}
Let $V=\Z_p v_0\oplus\Z_p v_1$, where $\Z_p$ is the ring of $p$-adic integers, and let $f$ act on $V$ by $fv_0=v_1$, $fv_1=0$; give $v_0$ weight $1$ and $v_1$ weight $-1$. Identify $B_m\otimes\Z_p$ with $V^{\otimes m}$ by $S\mapsto v_{\mathbf 1_S}$. Then $U$ is the action of $f$ on the tensor power, a subset of size $s$ has weight $m-2s$, and so $H=(m-h)/2$ with $h$ the weight. The divided powers $U^{(r)}$ (add $r$ new elements, in all ways) are integral, and so is the analogous operator $e^{(r)}$ (remove $r$ elements). Hence $B_m\otimes\Z_p$ is a module over the distribution algebra $\mathrm{Dist}(SL_2)_{\Z_p}$, and
\[
q(c+H)+U \;=\; q\big(c+\tfrac{m-h}{2}\big)+f
\]
is built from that action alone.

We use three standard facts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(T1)] $V^{\otimes m}$ is a tilting module, and it decomposes over $\Z_p$ as $\bigoplus_\lambda T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)^{n_\lambda}$, where $T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ is the unique lift of the indecomposable tilting module $T(\lambda)$ over $\F_p$. The reason is that $\mathrm{Ext}^1(\Delta,\nabla)=0$, so decompositions over $\F_p$ lift; this is classical \cite[\S1.2]{And}, \cite[II.E.6, II.E.19--E.22]{Jan}. For $p=2$ the argument is written out, and checked in Lean, in \cite[Lemma~3.3, Corollary~3.4]{DST}.
\item[(T2)] Let $\lambda\le p^2+p-1$. If $\lambda\le p-1$, or if $\lambda+1\equiv0\pmod p$ and $\lambda\le p^2-1$, then $T(\lambda)=\Delta(\lambda)$ is simple. Otherwise write
\[
\lambda=p-1+r+pa,\qquad 1\le r\le p,\ 0\le a\le p-1,
\]
where $r=p$ (and then $a=p-1$) occurs only for $\lambda=p^2+p-1$. Then $T(\lambda)$ has exactly two Weyl factors, $\Delta(\lambda)$ and $\Delta(\lambda^-)$ with $\lambda^-:=\lambda-2r$, and it is a non-split extension $0\to\Delta(\lambda)\to T(\lambda)\to\Delta(\lambda^-)\to0$; so is its lift over $\Z_p$. Indeed, by Donkin's tensor product theorem \cite[Prop.~2.1]{Don} (for $SL_2$ in every characteristic see \cite[Prop.~3.4(a)]{STWZ} and \cite[Remark~1.1(ii)]{And}), $T(p-1+r'+pb)\cong T(p-1+r')\otimes T(b)^{[1]}$ for $0\le r'\le p-1$. For $r\le p-1$ take $r'=r$, $b=a\le p-1$, so that $T(a)=\Delta(a)$. For $\lambda=p^2+p-1$ take $r'=0$, $b=p$: then $T(p-1)$ is the Steinberg module and $T(p)$ has the Weyl factors $\Delta(p),\Delta(p-2)$. In both cases the product of characters, expanded by Brauer's formula with $\chi(-n-2)=-\chi(n)$, leaves exactly $\chi(\lambda)+\chi(\lambda-2r)$; the other terms cancel in pairs.
\item[(T3)] Comparing characters, the multiplicities in $V^{\otimes m}$ are given, from the top down, by $n_\lambda=\beta(m,\lambda)-n_{\lambda^+}$. Here $\lambda^+$ is the weight whose bottom factor is $\lambda$, and $n_{\lambda^+}=0$ if there is none or if $\lambda^+>m$. (For $\lambda\le p^2+p-1$ the map $\lambda\mapsto\lambda^-$ is injective: $\lambda^-\equiv p-1-r\pmod p$ determines $r$, and then $a$.)
\end{enumerate}

For $\lambda\equiv m\pmod 2$, $0\le\lambda\le m$, write $\beta(m,\lambda)=\binom{m}{(m-\lambda)/2}-\binom m{(m-\lambda)/2-1}$ (the number of strings of length $\lambda+1$).

\subsection{The blocks}
Fix $m$, $c$ and $\lambda\equiv m\pmod2$, put $k=(m-\lambda)/2$ and
\[
v_t=e+v_p(c+k+t)\qquad(0\le t\le\lambda),
\]
with $v_0=\infty$ when $c=k=0$. Put $A=\sum_{t=0}^{\lambda}v_t$. When $T(\lambda)$ has two Weyl factors, with $(r,a)$ as in (T2), put also
\[
A'=\sum_{t=r}^{r+\lambda^-}v_t,\qquad \alpha=\sum_{t=(a+1)p}^{\lambda}v_t,\qquad \tau=1+[a=p-1],
\]
and
\[
E_{11}=A-\alpha-a,\qquad E_{12}=A'-a+\tau,\qquad E_{22}=A'-a+\alpha.
\]
Here $A'$ is the sum over the levels of the bottom factor (it is $A_c(m,\lambda^-)$ in the notation of Theorem~\ref{glue}), and $\alpha$ is the sum over the last $r$ levels of the top factor. Define the abelian $p$-group $G_c(m,\lambda)$ by
\[
G_c(m,\lambda)=\begin{cases}
\Z/p^{A}, & \lambda\le p-1,\\[2pt]
(\Z/p)^{a}\oplus\Z/p^{A-a}, & \lambda+1=(a+1)p\le p^2,\\[2pt]
(\Z/p)^{2a}\oplus\Z/p^{x}\oplus\Z/p^{y}, & \text{two Weyl factors},
\end{cases}
\]
where $x=\min(E_{11},E_{12},E_{22})$ and $y=E_{11}+E_{22}-x$. When $c=k=0$ (the block containing the empty set) we put instead: $\Z_p$ for $\lambda\le p-1$; $\Z_p\oplus(\Z/p)^a$ in the second case; and $\Z_p\oplus(\Z/p)^{2a}\oplus\Z/p^{\min(E_{12},E_{22})}$ in the third.

When $a+1<p$ one has $\tau=1$ and $x=E_{12}=A'+1-a$, so the third case reads $(\Z/p)^{2a}\oplus\Z/p^{A'+1-a}\oplus\Z/p^{A-1-a}$, which is the block of version~2. When $a=p-1$ all three numbers $E_{ij}$ can be the minimum: for $p=3$, $\lambda=10$, $k=0$, $e=1$ the pairs $(x,y)$ are $(11,11)$ for $c=1$ and $(10,12)$ for $c=2$.

The first theorem is the statement conjectured in version~1 of this paper. It is the case $m\le2p-2$ of Theorem~\ref{thG} below, where only $a=0$ occurs.

\begin{theorem}[the Glue Theorem; Conjecture F of version 1]\label{glue}
Let $p$ be a prime, $q$ an integer with $e=v_p(q)\ge1$, $m\le2p-2$ and $c\ge0$. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}\Syl_pX_q(m,c)\;\cong\;&\bigoplus_{\lambda\le p-1}\big(\Z/p^{A_c(m,\lambda)}\big)^{\beta(m,\lambda)-\beta'(m,\lambda)}\\&\oplus\bigoplus_{p\le\lambda\le m}\Big(\Z/p^{A_c(m,\lambda)-1}\oplus\Z/p^{A_c(m,2p-2-\lambda)+1}\Big)^{\beta(m,\lambda)},\end{aligned}
\]
where $A_c(m,\lambda)=\sum_{r=(m-\lambda)/2}^{(m+\lambda)/2}\big(e+v_p(c+r)\big)$, $\beta'(m,\lambda)=\beta(m,2p-2-\lambda)$ if $\lambda\le p-2$ and $\beta'(m,p-1)=0$, and $\lambda$ runs over integers $\equiv m\pmod2$. When $c=0$ the string of length $m+1$ (through the empty set) has a zero on its diagonal: for $\lambda=m<p$ it gives nothing, and for $\lambda=m\ge p$ the term $\Z/p^{A_c(m,m)-1}$ is omitted (it is the free summand) while its partner $\Z/p^{A_0(m,2p-2-m)+1}$ stays.
\end{theorem}

More generally:

\begin{theorem}[two Weyl factors]\label{thG}
Let $p$ be a prime, $q$ an integer with $e=v_p(q)\ge1$, $c\ge0$, and $m\le p^2+p-1$. Then
\[
\Z_p^{\,[c=0]}\oplus\Syl_pX_q(m,c)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\lambda}G_c(m,\lambda)^{\,n_\lambda},
\]
where $\lambda$ runs over the integers $\equiv m\pmod 2$ with $0\le\lambda\le m$ and $n_\lambda$ is as in (T3).
\end{theorem}

With Theorem~\ref{thA}, Theorem~\ref{thG} determines $\Syl_pK(H(d,q))$ for every $d\le p^2+p-1$. This contains Conjecture~H of version~2 ($p$ odd, $d\le p^2-2$). For $p=3$ the range is $d\le11$ and for $p=5$ it is $d\le29$. For $p=2$ it is $d\le5$; the prime $2$ is treated for every $d$ in Section~\ref{sec:two}. The range is exactly the range in which every tilting summand of $V^{\otimes m}$ has at most two Weyl factors; at $m=p^2+p$ the block $\lambda=p^2+p$, with four Weyl factors, appears. We claim nothing for $p\nmid q$, where the answer is \cite[Theorem~2]{JNR}. (Version~3 said here that the formula fails for $p\nmid q$, at $(q,p,d)=(4,3,4)$. That was wrong: evaluated at $e=0$ the formula gives the right group there, and in all $784$ pieces with $p\nmid q$ and $p\le5$ checked by the independent reader. We have not proved it for $e=0$.)

\subsection{Proofs}
Fix $\lambda$ with two Weyl factors, $(r,a)$ as in (T2), $\mu=\lambda^-$. Let $\ell_i=f^{(i)}x$ ($0\le i\le\lambda$) be the standard basis of $\Delta_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$, so $f\ell_i=(i+1)\ell_{i+1}$, $e\ell_i=(\lambda-i+1)\ell_{i-1}$, and more generally $f^{(t)}\ell_i=\binom{i+t}{t}\ell_{i+t}$ and $e^{(t)}\ell_i=\binom{\lambda-i+t}{t}\ell_{i-t}$. Let $s_j=f^{(j)}y$ ($0\le j\le\mu$) be that of $\Delta_{\Z_p}(\mu)$. Put
\[
\nu=\min_{1\le t\le r}v_p\tbinom{\lambda-r+t}{t}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[the glue is forced]\label{forced}
$\nu=2$ if $r\le p-1$ and $a=p-1$, and $\nu=1$ otherwise. $T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ is isomorphic to the lattice in $\Delta_{\Q_p}(\lambda)\oplus\Delta_{\Q_p}(\mu)$ spanned by the $\ell_i$ and by
\[
\tilde s_j=s_j+p^{-\nu}\tbinom{j+r}{j}\,\ell_{j+r}\qquad(0\le j\le\mu).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The value of $\nu$ follows from Kummer's theorem. If $r\le p-1$, then $\lambda-r=(a+1)p-1$, and adding $t\in[1,r]$ to it carries once out of the units digit; it carries a second time exactly when $a+1=p$. If $r=p$, then $\lambda-r=p^2-1$: adding $t<p$ carries twice, while adding $t=p$ carries once.

By (T2), $T=T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ contains $\Delta_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ with quotient $\Delta_{\Z_p}(\mu)$. Since $T\cap\Delta_{\Q_p}(\lambda)=\Delta_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ and the weight spaces of $\Delta(\lambda)$ are one-dimensional, the lifts of the $s_j$ to $T$ can be chosen of the form $\tilde s_j=s_j+x_j\ell_{j+r}$ with $x_j\in\Q_p$, unique modulo $\Z_p$.

Stability under $f$ gives $(j+r+1)x_j-(j+1)x_{j+1}\in\Z_p$. Stability under $f^{(p)}$ at $j=kp$, for $(k+1)p\le\mu$, gives
\[\tbinom{(k+1)p+r}{p}x_{kp}-\tbinom{(k+1)p}{p}x_{(k+1)p}\in\Z_p.\]
In the range of (T2), $\mu\le p^2-2$, so $k+1\le p-1$ and $\binom{(k+1)p}{p}\equiv k+1$ is a unit; so is $\binom{(k+1)p+r}{p}$, which is $\equiv k+1$ if $r\le p-1$ and $\equiv k+2$ if $r=p$ (and then $\mu=p^2-p-1$, so $k+2\le p-1$). Inside a segment $[kp,(k+1)p-1]$ the factor $j+1$ is a unit, so $x_j\equiv\binom{j+r}{j}\binom{kp+r}{kp}^{-1}x_{kp}$ modulo $\Z_p$. Across segments, $x_{(k+1)p}\equiv\binom{(k+1)p+r}{p}\binom{(k+1)p}{p}^{-1}x_{kp}$, and
\[\tbinom{n+r}{p}\tbinom np^{-1}=\tbinom{n+r}{n}\tbinom{n-p+r}{n-p}^{-1}\qquad(n=(k+1)p)\]
is an identity of rational numbers (both sides equal $\frac{(n+r)!\,(n-p)!}{n!\,(n-p+r)!}$). Hence $x_j\equiv\binom{j+r}{j}x_0$ modulo $\Z_p$ for every $j$, and the lattice is determined by $x_0$.

Stability under $e^{(t)}$ at $j=0$ gives $x_0\binom{\lambda-r+t}{t}\ell_{r-t}\in T$ for $1\le t\le r$, so $v_p(x_0)\ge-\nu$. Suppose $v_p(x_0)\ge1-\nu$. Then $e^{(t)}\tilde s_0\in pT$ for every $t\ge1$ (also for $t>r$, where it vanishes), so the image of $\tilde s_0$ in $T\otimes\F_p$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\mu$. It generates the image of a map $\Delta(\mu)\to T\otimes\F_p$, and the composite with $T\otimes\F_p\to\Delta(\mu)$ is the identity on the generator, so it is an isomorphism and the extension splits. This is impossible, because $T\otimes\F_p=T(\lambda)$ is indecomposable. So $x_0=u\,p^{-\nu}$ with $u$ a unit, and rescaling $y$ makes $u=1$. The lattice so described is $T$ itself, so it is stable under all $f^{(t)}$ and $e^{(t)}$; nothing more has to be checked. (For $f^{(t)}$ this is also visible directly: $f^{(t)}\tilde s_j-\binom{j+t}{t}\tilde s_{j+t}$ vanishes by the identity $\binom{j+r}{j}\binom{j+r+t}{t}=\binom{j+t}{t}\binom{j+t+r}{j+t}$.) Stability under every $e^{(t)}$ and $f^{(t)}$ is also checked by machine in Section~\ref{sec:ver}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the blocks]\label{blocks}
Embed $T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ in $B_m\otimes\Z_p$ at levels $k,\dots,k+\lambda$ ($m=\lambda+2k$). Then the cokernel of $q(c+H)+U$ on it is $G_c(m,\lambda)$. The same holds for the simple blocks.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $N_t=q(c+k+t)$, so $v_p(N_t)=v_t$, and $M=q(c+H)+U$. In the basis of Lemma~\ref{forced}:
\begin{itemize}
\item $M\ell_i=N_i\ell_i+(i+1)\ell_{i+1}$;
\item $M\tilde s_j=N_{j+r}\tilde s_j+(j+1)\tilde s_{j+1}$ for $j<\mu$; the $\ell$-component is $p^{-\nu}[(j+r+1)\binom{j+r}{j}-(j+1)\binom{j+r+1}{j+1}]\ell_{j+r+1}=0$;
\item $M\tilde s_\mu=N_{\mu+r}\tilde s_\mu+u\,\ell_{\mu+r+1}$, where $\mu+r+1=(a+1)p$ and $u=p^{-\nu}\binom{\mu+r}{\mu}(a+1)p$.
\end{itemize}
By Lucas, $\binom{\mu+r}{\mu}$ is a unit: $\mu+r=(a+1)p-1$ has digits $(a,p-1)$, or $(p-1,p-1)$ when $r=p$. So $v_p(u)=v_p((a+1)p)-\nu$. This is $0$ when $r\le p-1$, and $1$ when $r=p$.

The non-unit subdiagonal entries are:
\begin{itemize}
\item on the $\ell$-chain, the $i+1=tp$ with $1\le t\le a+1$, of valuation $1$ except the last, of valuation $\tau$;
\item on the $\tilde s$-chain, the $j+1=tp\le\mu$, of valuation $1$. There are $a$ of them if $r\le p-1$, and $a-1$ if $r=p$.
\end{itemize}
Solving the unit subdiagonals, the cokernel is generated by the first vector of each segment between them, with one relation at the end of each segment. Write $g_t=\ell_{tp}$ and $h_t=\tilde s_{tp}$, and let $\alpha_t$, $\sigma_t$ be the sums of the $v$'s over the segments, so that $\sum_t\alpha_t=A$, $\sum_t\sigma_t=A'$ and the last $\alpha_t$ is $\alpha$. After rescaling the generators by units, the relations are:
\begin{itemize}
\item $p^{\alpha_t}g_t+p\,g_{t+1}$ on the $\ell$-chain, the last link having $p^{\tau}$ instead of $p$;
\item $p^{\alpha}g_{a+1}$ at the end of the $\ell$-chain;
\item $p^{\sigma_t}h_t+p\,h_{t+1}$ on the $\tilde s$-chain;
\item at the end of the $\tilde s$-chain, $p^{\sigma}h_{\rm last}+p^{v_p(u)}g_{a+1}$.
\end{itemize}
If $r=p$, every entry has valuation $\ge1$; if $r\le p-1$ this holds after the elimination of $g_{a+1}$ described next. In both cases the relation matrix is then $pM'$ with $M'$ integral.

If $r\le p-1$ the last relation has a unit coefficient on $g_{a+1}$. Eliminating $g_{a+1}$ turns the last link of the $\ell$-chain into $p^{\tau+\sigma_a}h_a$ and the end relation into $p^{\alpha+\sigma_a}h_a$. In $M'$ the links with coefficient $1$ form $a$ units on the $\ell$-chain and $a$ on the $\tilde s$-chain, in distinct rows and columns. Clearing them (column operations along each chain, then row operations) splits off $I_{2a}$. The $2\times2$ matrix that remains, on the rows of the last link of the $\ell$-chain and of the end relation $p^{\alpha+\sigma_a}h_a$, and on the columns $g_0,h_0$, is upper triangular with diagonal valuations $E_{11}-1$, $E_{22}-1$ and corner $E_{12}-1$.

If $r=p$ the $\tilde s$-chain has $a-1$ unit links and its end relation has a unit coefficient on $g_{a+1}=g_p$ in $M'$. These give $2a$ units again, and clearing them leaves the same $2\times2$ matrix.

So the relation matrix is equivalent to $pI_{2a}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}p^{E_{11}}&\ast\,p^{E_{12}}\\0&p^{E_{22}}\end{pmatrix}$, with a unit $\ast$. The Smith form of such a matrix is $(p^{x},p^{E_{11}+E_{22}-x})$ with $x$ the least of the three valuations, and this is $G_c(m,\lambda)$.

When $c=k=0$, $N_0=0$: the column $g_0$ is zero and gives the free summand, and what remains on $h_0$ are the relations $p^{E_{12}}$ and $p^{E_{22}}$. The simple blocks are single chains. When $\lambda+1=(a+1)p\le p^2$ the chain has $a$ non-unit subdiagonal entries, $i+1=tp$ with $1\le t\le a\le p-1$, each of valuation $1$. The same elimination gives $(\Z/p)^a\oplus\Z/p^{A-a}$.

When $a+1<p$ we have $E_{12}\le E_{22}$ (since $\alpha\ge1$) and $E_{12}\le E_{11}$, because $A-\alpha=A'+\sum_{t<r}v_t\ge A'+1$. So $x=E_{12}$ and $y=A-1-a$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorems~\ref{thG} and~\ref{glue}]
Combine (T1)--(T3), Lemma~\ref{forced} and Lemma~\ref{blocks}. For $m\le p^2+p-1$ every $\lambda\le m$ is simple or has two Weyl factors, by (T2).
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
For $p=q=3$ and $d=4$: Theorem~\ref{thA} gives the pieces $X(4,0)$, $X(3,1)^4$, $X(2,2)^6$, $X(1,3)^4$, $X(0,4)$, and Theorem~\ref{glue} gives $\Syl_3K(H(4,3))\cong(\Z/3)^2\oplus(\Z/3^2)^{7}\oplus(\Z/3^3)^{8}\oplus(\Z/3^4)^{17}$, which is the group we computed.
\end{example}

\section{The prime $2$}\label{sec:two}

For $p=2$ the blocks are the families of \cite{DST}, and \cite{DST} computes them for every family and every power of $2$. This gives the whole $2$-part of $K(H(d,q))$ for every $d$ and every even $q$.

For a family $\lambda\ge1$ write $\lambda+1=2^{\theta}+\sum_{t\in I}2^t$ with $I\subseteq\{0,\dots,\theta-1\}$, $\Theta=2^\theta$, $h_t=\mathrm{next}(t)-t$ (with $\mathrm{next}(t)$ the next element of $I\cup\{\theta\}$), $o_D=\sum_{t\in D}2^t$ for $D\subseteq I$, and $\kappa(a,b)=v_2\binom{a+b}{a}$. Following \cite[§3.4]{DST} put
\[
X^{(\alpha)}(\lambda,i)=\coker\big(f+2^\alpha(D_\lambda+i)\ \text{on}\ T_{\Z_2}(\lambda)\big)\qquad(\alpha\ge1,\ i\ge0),
\]
where $D_\lambda$ is the floor operator $(\lambda-h)/2$. By \cite[Theorem~D, Proposition~5.4, Theorem~7.10]{DST}, which are stated and proved for every $\alpha\ge1$,
\[
X^{(\alpha)}(\lambda,i)\cong\bigoplus_{b=1}^{\theta-1}\big(\Z/2^b\big)^{2^{|I|+\theta-1-b}}\oplus\bigoplus_{D\subseteq I}\Z/2^{\,\theta+\alpha\Theta+\kappa(i-1,\Theta)+\hat y_D}\qquad(i\ge1),
\]
where $\hat y$ is the integer antitonic fit \cite[§1.2]{DST}, in the order of increasing $o_D$, of the clocks
\[
x_D=R_{i-1}(D)-R_{i-1+\Theta}(I\setminus D),\qquad R_\mu(E)=\sum_{t\in E}\big(h_t-\alpha2^t\big)-\kappa(\mu,o_E).
\]
For $i=0$ the summand $D=\emptyset$ is $\Z_2$, and the others are $\Z/2^{\theta+\alpha\Theta+\hat y^\sharp_D}$, with $\hat y^\sharp$ the fit of $x^\sharp_D=R^\sharp(D)-R^\sharp(I\setminus D)$, $R^\sharp(E)=\sum_{t\in E}(h_t-\alpha2^t)-\kappa(\Theta-1,o_E)$, over $D\ne\emptyset$. For $\lambda=0$, $X^{(\alpha)}(0,i)=\Z/2^{\alpha+v_2(i)}$ ($\Z_2$ if $i=0$). Let $m_\lambda(n)$ be the multiplicity of $T(\lambda)$ in $V^{\otimes n}$ in characteristic $2$ \cite[§1.2, Theorem~3.6]{DST}.

\begin{theorem}[the prime $2$]\label{thP}
Let $q$ be even, $e=v_2(q)$, $m\ge0$ and $c\ge0$. Then
\[
\Z_2^{\,[c=0]}\oplus\Syl_2X_q(m,c)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\lambda}X^{(e)}\big(\lambda,\,c+\tfrac{m-\lambda}{2}\big)^{m_\lambda(m)}.
\]
Consequently, for every $d\ge1$,
\[
\Z_2\oplus\Syl_2K\big(H(d,q)\big)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{j=0}^{d}\ \bigoplus_{\lambda}X^{(e)}\big(\lambda,\,j+\tfrac{d-j-\lambda}{2}\big)^{\binom dj(q-2)^j\,m_\lambda(d-j)} .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By (T1) for $p=2$ \cite[Theorem~3.6]{DST}, $B_m\otimes\Z_2=V^{\otimes m}=\bigoplus_\lambda T_{\Z_2}(\lambda)^{m_\lambda(m)}$ as $\mathrm{Dist}$-modules. On a summand $T(\lambda)$, placed at the levels $k=(m-\lambda)/2,\dots,k+\lambda$, a vector of weight $h$ has $H=(m-h)/2=k+D_\lambda$, so $q(c+H)+U=f+q(c+k+D_\lambda)$. Write $q=2^eu$ with $u$ odd. The proof of Lemma~\ref{profile} applies verbatim to any lattice graded by the floor on which $f$ raises the floor by one; it rescales the floors by units and turns $f+2^eu(D_\lambda+i)$ into $f+2^e(D_\lambda+i)$. So the cokernel on the summand is $X^{(e)}(\lambda,c+k)$. The second statement follows from Theorem~\ref{thA}.
\end{proof}

For $q=2$ only $j=0$ survives, $e=1$, and this is the Main Theorem of \cite{DST}. For $e\ge1$ arbitrary, the theorem was checked against the Smith forms of $q(c+H)+U$ on $B_m$ in $384$ cases with $m\le11$, $e\le4$, $c\le4$ (the list is in the logs of the record) and against the full Laplacians of $H(d,q)$ for $(d,q)=(3,4),(4,4),(3,6),(3,8),(2,12),(2,16),(3,10)$. Two controls fail: using $\alpha=1$ instead of $\alpha=e$ changes the answer in all $112$ cases with $e\ge2$ tried, and omitting the antitonic fit changes it in $16$ of $128$.

\section{The whole group: Theorems I and J}\label{sec:conjI}

For a prime $p$, $\lambda\ge0$, $i\ge0$ and $e\ge1$ put
\[
Y_p(\lambda,i,e)=\coker\big(f+p^{e}(D_\lambda+i)\ \text{on}\ T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)\big),
\]
with $D_\lambda=(\lambda-h)/2$ the floor operator, as in Section~\ref{sec:two}. By (T1), and by Lemma~\ref{profile} applied as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thP}, for every $m$ and $c$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blocks}
\Z_p^{\,[c=0]}\oplus\Syl_pX_q(m,c)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_\lambda Y_p\big(\lambda,\,c+\tfrac{m-\lambda}2,\,v_p(q)\big)^{n_\lambda(m)},
\end{equation}
where $n_\lambda(m)$ is the multiplicity of $T(\lambda)$ in $V^{\otimes m}$. So everything comes down to the groups $Y_p(\lambda,i,e)$. Theorem~\ref{thG} computes them for $\lambda\le p^2+p-1$, and Theorem~\ref{thP} for $p=2$. In this section we compute them for every $\lambda$ and every odd $p$.

\subsection{Digits, dancers, clocks}
Fix a prime $p$ and $\lambda\ge0$, and write
\[
\lambda+1=\sum_{t=0}^{k}d_tp^t,\qquad 0\le d_t\le p-1,\ d_k\ne0,\qquad I=I(\lambda)=\{t<k:d_t\ne0\},\qquad K=d_kp^k .
\]
For $t\in I$ put $\mathrm{next}(t)=\min\{u\in I\cup\{k\}:u>t\}$ and $h_t=\mathrm{next}(t)-t$. The subsets $D\subseteq I$ are the \emph{dancers}; put $o_D=\sum_{t\in D}d_tp^t$. Since $d_tp^t>\sum_{u<t}(p-1)p^u$, the order of the dancers by increasing $o_D$ (the \emph{o-order}) is the lexicographic order of the digit sets from the top, exactly as for $p=2$; and $o_{I\setminus D}=o_I-o_D$, so $D\mapsto I\setminus D$ reverses it. Let $\kappa_p(a,b)=v_p\binom{a+b}{a}$, the number of carries in the base-$p$ addition $a+b$ (Kummer). For $E\subseteq I$ and $\mu\ge0$ put
\[
R_\mu(E)=\sum_{t\in E}\big(h_t-e\,d_tp^t\big)-\kappa_p(\mu,o_E),\qquad R^\sharp(E)=\sum_{t\in E}\big(h_t-e\,d_tp^t\big)-(k-\min E)\quad(R^\sharp(\emptyset)=0).
\]
The \emph{clocks} are: for $i\ge1$, with $m=i-1$, $x_D=R_m(D)-R_{m+K}(I\setminus D)$ ($D\subseteq I$); for $i=0$, $x^\sharp_D=R^\sharp(D)-R^\sharp(I\setminus D)$ ($D\ne\emptyset$). Let $\hat y_D$ and $\hat y^\sharp_D$ be their \emph{integer antitonic fits} in the o-order: partition the positions into consecutive blocks by the pool-adjacent-violators algorithm, so that the block means are strictly decreasing and each block value is the mean of its entries (the antitonic least-squares fit); then replace a block of $b$ positions with sum $S$ by $S\bmod b$ copies of $\lceil S/b\rceil$ followed by $b-(S\bmod b)$ copies of $\lfloor S/b\rfloor$. (By Lemma~\ref{integral} below the rounding never acts.) For $p=2$ ($d_t=1$, $K=2^k$) this is exactly the notation of \cite[\S1.2, \S5]{DST} with $\alpha=e$.

\subsection{The theorems}
\begin{mdframed}[style=headline]
\begin{theorem}[Conjecture~I of version~3]\label{thI}
Let $p$ be a prime, $\lambda\ge0$, $i\ge0$ and $e\ge1$, and let $k$, $d_k$ and $I$ be as above. Then exactly $2^{|I|}$ of the cyclic summands of $Y_p(\lambda,i,e)$ have order larger than $p^k$ (a free summand, when $i=0$, counting as one of them). The others have order at most $p^k$, and they form the group
\[
S_p(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{h=1}^{k-1}\big(\Z/p^h\big)^{(p-1)d_kp^{k-h-1}2^{|I|}}\;\oplus\;\big(\Z/p^k\big)^{(d_k-1)2^{|I|}},
\]
which depends only on $p$ and the base-$p$ digits of $\lambda+1$, not on $i$ or $e$.
\end{theorem}
\end{mdframed}

The number of large summands is the number of Weyl factors of $T(\lambda)$ (Lemma~\ref{dance}(i)). The total number of cyclic summands, $d_kp^{k-1}2^{|I|}=\dim T(\lambda)/p$ for $k\ge1$, is the number of Jordan blocks of $f$ on $T(\lambda)$ over $\F_p$; this is classical, since $T(\lambda)$ is projective over the first Frobenius kernel for $\lambda\ge p-1$ \cite[II.11.11]{Jan}.

\begin{mdframed}[style=headline]
\begin{theorem}[the whole group]\label{thJ}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, $\lambda\ge0$ and $e\ge1$. Then
\begin{align*}
Y_p(\lambda,i,e)&\;\cong\;S_p(\lambda)\oplus\bigoplus_{D\subseteq I}\Z/p^{\,k+eK+\kappa_p(i-1,K)+\hat y_D}\qquad(i\ge1),\\
Y_p(\lambda,0,e)&\;\cong\;S_p(\lambda)\oplus\Z_p\oplus\bigoplus_{\emptyset\ne D\subseteq I}\Z/p^{\,k+eK+\hat y^\sharp_D}.
\end{align*}
Consequently, for every prime $p$ dividing $q$, with $e=v_p(q)$, and every $d\ge1$,
\[
\Z_p\oplus\Syl_pK\big(H(d,q)\big)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{j=0}^{d}\ \bigoplus_{\lambda}Y_p\big(\lambda,\,j+\tfrac{d-j-\lambda}{2},\,e\big)^{\binom dj(q-2)^j\,n_\lambda(d-j)},
\]
$\lambda$ running over $0\le\lambda\le d-j$ with $\lambda\equiv d-j\pmod2$, where for $p=2$ the groups $Y_2=X^{(e)}$ are those of Theorem~\ref{thP}. The multiplicities $n_\lambda(m)$ are obtained by peeling the characters
\[
\operatorname{ch}T(\lambda)=\prod_{t<k}\chi_{p-1}\big(z^{p^t}\big)\,\big(z^{d_tp^t}+z^{-d_tp^t}\big)^{[d_t\ne0]}\cdot\chi_{d_k-1}\big(z^{p^k}\big),\qquad \chi_n(z)=\frac{z^{n+1}-z^{-n-1}}{z-z^{-1}},
\]
off $(z+z^{-1})^m$ from the top.
\end{theorem}
\end{mdframed}

For $\lambda\le p-2$ one has $k=0$, $I=\emptyset$, $K=\lambda+1<p$, and Theorem~\ref{thJ} gives $\Z/p^{e(\lambda+1)+\kappa_p(i-1,\lambda+1)}$, which is Theorem~\ref{thBprime}. For $\lambda\le p^2+p-1$ it agrees with Theorem~\ref{thG} (checked on $3450$ cells, \S\ref{sec:ver}), and at $p=2$ the same formula is Theorem~\ref{thP}. The right-hand side is a finite computation with binomial valuations; no Smith form is needed.

\begin{example}
The first block beyond Theorem~\ref{thG} is $\lambda=12$ for $p=3$: $\lambda+1=111_3$, $k=2$, $d_2=1$, $I=\{0,1\}$, $h_0=h_1=1$, four Weyl factors. Theorem~\ref{thI} gives $S_3(12)=(\Z/3)^8$, and Theorem~\ref{thJ} gives the large summands. For $q\in\{3,6\}$ ($e=1$), as computed directly from the Smith form of $q(c+H)+U$ on $B_{12}$ after removing the blocks $\lambda\le10$:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{>{$}c<{$}l}
\toprule
c & block $\lambda=12$\\
\midrule
0 & $\Z_3\oplus(\Z/3)^8\oplus\Z/3^{7}\oplus\Z/3^{10}\oplus\Z/3^{12}$\\
1,2 & $(\Z/3)^8\oplus(\Z/3^{9})^2\oplus(\Z/3^{13})^2$\\

Version~3 stated Conjecture~I after the cell $c=0$ alone and predicted the other seven cells; all held. Later, four cells with $q=9$ ($e=2$) were computed directly on $B_{12}$, and agree with Theorem~\ref{thJ} in every exponent below $19$, the precision of that computation; and $31$ further pieces $X_q(m,c)$, predicted from Theorem~\ref{thJ} before they were computed ($23$ with $p=3$ and $m\in\{12,13\}$, $8$ with $p=2$), agree exactly (\S\ref{sec:ver}).
\end{example}

\section{Proofs of Theorems I and J: the odd dance}\label{sec:dance}

Throughout this section $p$ is odd, $A=\Z_p$, and $\Dist_A=A\otimes\Dist_\Z(SL_2)$ is the Kostant form, generated by the divided powers $E^{(r)},F^{(r)}$ and the $\binom{H}{r}$. A \emph{lattice} is a $\Dist_A$-module that is free of finite rank over $A$ and is the sum of its weight spaces, with integer weights. We put $u=-1$ (any unit $\equiv-1\pmod p$ would do) and, on a lattice, $X=FE$. As in Section~6, $\Delta_A(\mu)$ has the basis $e_s=F^{(s)}v$, $Fe_s=(s+1)e_{s+1}$. A lattice is \emph{graded by floors} when $F$ raises the floor by one; in $\Delta_A(\mu)$ the floor of $e_s$ is $s$. The plan is the one of \cite{DST} for $p=2$: explicit lattices built one base-$p$ digit at a time (\S9.1--9.2), elimination of unit pivots digit by digit (\S9.3), and the Smith form of the matrix that remains (\S9.4).

\subsection{The twist and the split}

\begin{lemma}\label{EFH}
Let $X,Y$ be finite-dimensional $U_\Q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-modules and $\varphi\colon X\to Y$ linear with $\varphi F=F\varphi$ and $\varphi H=H\varphi$. Then $\varphi E=E\varphi$. Hence a weight-preserving $A$-isomorphism of lattices that commutes with $F$ is an isomorphism of lattices, and on a vector space with given $F$, $H$ there is at most one $E$ making it an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\Delta:=\varphi E-E\varphi$ raises weights by $2$ and commutes with $F$, since $\varphi[E,F]-[E,F]\varphi=\varphi H-H\varphi=0$. By Weyl's complete reducibility, $X$ is spanned by vectors $F^jx$ with $Ex=0$, $x$ of weight $h\ge0$, $F^{h+1}x=0$, and $Y_\Q$ is a sum of simple modules $\Delta_\Q(c)$; on each of them $F^{h+1}$ is injective on the weight space $h+2$. As $F^{h+1}\Delta x=\Delta F^{h+1}x=0$, $\Delta x=0$, and $\Delta F^jx=F^j\Delta x=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[the twist $\Phi_p$, digit $0$]\label{defPhi}
For a lattice $N$ let $\Phi_p(N)=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{p-1}b_j\otimes N$ (written $b_jn$), and for $n$ of weight $w$:
$H(b_jn)=(pw+p-1-2j)\,b_jn$; $F(b_jn)=b_{j+1}n$ for $j\le p-2$ and $F(b_{p-1}n)=pu\,b_0(Fn)$; $E(b_jn)=b_{j-1}\,g_jn$ for $j\ge1$, with $g_j=p^2X+j(pw+p-j)$, and $E(b_0n)=(p/u)\,b_{p-1}(En)$. On morphisms $\Phi_p(f)=1\otimes f$. If $n$ is on floor $f$, then $b_jn$ is on floor $pf+j$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{propPhi}
(a) $\Phi_p(N)$ is a lattice, and $\Phi_p$ is an exact functor that preserves saturated sublattices. For $r\ge0$, with $c=\lfloor(j+r)/p\rfloor$,
\begin{gather*}
F^{(r)}(b_jn)=(pu)^c\tfrac{c!}{r!}\,b_{j+r-pc}F^{(c)}n,\\
E^{(r)}(b_jn)=(p/u)^{c'}\tfrac{c'!}{r!}\,b_{j-r+pc'}\,E^{(c')}\!\!\prod_{(a,i)\in S(j,r)}\!\!\big[p^2X+(i-pa)(p(w+1+a)-i)\big]n,
\end{gather*}
where $c'=\lfloor(r+p-1-j)/p\rfloor$ is the number of wraps and $S(j,r)$ is the set of pairs (wraps already made $a$, current index $i\ge1$) met in the first $r$ steps $j,j-1,\dots,1$, then $p-1,\dots,1$, and so on; all coefficients are $p$-integral.
(b) $\Phi_p(N)\otimes\F_p\cong St\otimes(N\otimes\F_p)^{[1]}$, with $St=\Delta(p-1)$ the Steinberg module.
(c) $\Phi_p(\Delta_A(\mu))\cong\Delta_A(p\mu+p-1)$, $\Phi_p(\nabla_A(\mu))\cong\nabla_A(p\mu+p-1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) The relation $[E,F]=H$ is checked on each $b_jn$: for $1\le j\le p-2$, $g_{j+1}-g_j=pw+p-1-2j$; on $b_0n$, $g_1-p^2X=pw+p-1$; on $b_{p-1}n$, $p^2(X+w)-g_{p-1}=pw-p+1$. These are the weights, and $E$, $F$ move weights by $\pm2$. The formula for $F^{(r)}$ follows from $F^r(b_jn)=(pu)^cb_{j+r-pc}F^cn$; the one for $E^{(r)}$ by walking $E$ down the indices, using that the Casimir $\Omega=4FE+(H+1)^2$ of $\Phi_p(N)$ is $p^2\Omega_N$ on every $b_jn$, so that $g_j$ is $(p^2\Omega_N-(p(w+1)-2j)^2)/4$. Integrality: $v_p(p^cc!/r!)=c+v_p(c!)-\lfloor r/p\rfloor-v_p(\lfloor r/p\rfloor!)\ge0$ because $c\ge\lfloor r/p\rfloor$ and $x\mapsto x+v_p(x!)$ is increasing (Legendre); the same for $E$ with $c'\ge\lfloor r/p\rfloor$. Exactness and saturation hold because $\Phi_p(N)=N^p$ as $A$-modules and $1\otimes f$ commutes with $F$, $H$, hence with $E$ (Lemma~\ref{EFH}).
(b) Modulo $p$, with $r=ps+t$, $0\le t\le p-1$, the formulas of (a) reduce to $F^{(r)}(b_jn)\equiv[j+t\le p-1]\,\frac1{t!}\,b_{j+t}F^{(s)}n$ (using $(ps+t)!/(p^ss!)\equiv(-1)^st!$ and $u\equiv-1$) and $E^{(r)}(b_jn)\equiv[t\le j]\,(-1)^t\big(\frac{j!}{(j-t)!}\big)^2\frac1{t!}\,b_{j-t}E^{(s)}n$ (a full cycle of $g$'s is $\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}(-i^2)\equiv1$). These are the formulas of $St\otimes\bar N^{[1]}$ computed with the coproduct, in the basis $b'_j=F^jb'_0$ of $St$.
(c) In the basis $x_R=b_je_s$, $R=ps+j$, one has $Fx_R=x_{R+1}$ for $j\le p-2$ and $Fx_{ps+p-1}=pu(s+1)x_{p(s+1)}$, while in $\Delta(p\mu+p-1)$, $Fe_R=(R+1)e_{R+1}$, with $R+1$ a unit unless $j=p-1$, where it is $p(s+1)$. A diagonal map with unit entries therefore commutes with $F$ and $H$, and Lemma~\ref{EFH} applies. The same for $\nabla$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[the split $\Psi_r$, digit $r$, $1\le r\le p-1$]\label{defPsi}
For a lattice $N$ let $\Psi_r(N)=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{p-1}(A_j\otimes N\oplus C_j\otimes N)$, with $H(A_jn)=(pw+p-1+r-2j)A_jn$, $H(C_jn)=(pw+p-1-r-2j)C_jn$; $F(A_jn)=A_{j+1}n$ and $F(C_jn)=C_{j+1}n$ for $j\le p-2$,
\[
F(A_{p-1}n)=pu\,A_0(Fn)+p\,C_{p-r}n,\qquad F(C_{p-1}n)=pu\,C_0(Fn);
\]
and, with $g^A_j=p^2X+j(pw+p+r-j)$ and $g^C_j=p^2X+j(pw+p-r-j)$:
\begin{align*}
E(A_0n)&=(p/u)\,A_{p-1}(En)-(p-r)(p/u)\,C_{p-1-r}(En),\\
E(A_jn)&=A_{j-1}g^A_jn+r(p/u)\,C_{p+j-1-r}(En)\quad(1\le j\le r),\\
E(A_jn)&=A_{j-1}g^A_jn+rp^2\,C_{j-1-r}(Xn)\quad(r+1\le j\le p-1),\\
E(C_0n)&=r\,A_{r-1}n+(p/u)\,C_{p-1}(En),\\
E(C_jn)&=C_{j-1}g^C_jn+r\,A_{j+r-1}n\quad(1\le j\le p-r),\\
E(C_jn)&=C_{j-1}(g^C_j+rp)n+rpu\,A_{j+r-1-p}(Fn)\quad(p-r+1\le j\le p-1).
\end{align*}
On morphisms $\Psi_r(f)=1\otimes f$. If $n$ is on floor $f$, then $A_jn$ is on floor $pf+j$ and $C_jn$ on floor $pf+r+j$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{propPsi}
(a) $\Psi_r(N)$ is a lattice, and $\Psi_r$ is an exact functor that preserves saturated sublattices.
(b) $\Psi_r(N)\otimes\F_p\cong T(p-1+r)\otimes(N\otimes\F_p)^{[1]}$.
(c) $\Psi_r(\Delta_A(\mu))$ has a sublattice $\Delta_A(p\mu+p-1+r)$ with quotient $\Delta_A(p\mu+p-1-r)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) The relation $[E,F]=H$ is checked case by case on the ten index ranges of the definition (for instance, on $A_{p-1}n$ with $r\le p-2$ the $A$-coefficient of $EF-FE$ is $p^2EF+pr-g^A_{p-1}=pw-p+1+r$, the weight, and the $C$-coefficient is $0$). For $r=1$ the map $A_jn\mapsto\hat b_0\otimes b_jn+j\,\hat b_1\otimes b_{j-1}n$, $C_jn\mapsto\hat b_1\otimes b_jn$ is a unitriangular isomorphism $\Psi_1(N)\cong V\otimes\Phi_p(N)$ commuting with $F$ and $H$; by Lemma~\ref{EFH} it is an isomorphism, and $V\otimes\Phi_p(N)$ is a lattice. For $r\ge2$ the map $\iota\colon\Psi_r(N)\to V\otimes\Psi_{r-1}(N)$, $\iota(A_jn)=F^j\alpha(n)$, $\iota(C_jn)=F^j\gamma(n)$, with (primes for $\Psi_{r-1}$)
\[
\alpha(n)=\hat b_0\otimes A'_0n+(p/u)\,\hat b_1\otimes C'_{p-r}(En),\qquad \gamma(n)=\hat b_0\otimes C'_1n+\hat b_1\otimes A'_{r-1}n-(pw+p-r)\,\hat b_1\otimes C'_0n,
\]
commutes with $F$ (it suffices to check $F^p\gamma(n)=pu\,\gamma(Fn)$ and $F^p\alpha(n)=pu\,\alpha(Fn)+p\,F^{p-r}\gamma(n)$) and with $H$, hence with $E$ over $\Q$. Its reduction modulo $p$ is injective: on suitable coordinates it is diagonal with entries $1$ and $r-1$, a unit. So the image is a saturated $U_\Q$-stable sublattice of a lattice, hence $\Dist_A$-stable, and $\Psi_r(N)$ is a lattice by induction on $r$. Exactness as for $\Phi_p$.
(b) Induction on $r$; $r=1$ is $V\otimes St=T(p)$. For $r\ge2$, $\bar\iota$ embeds $\overline{\Psi_r(N)}$ in $V\otimes T(p-2+r)\otimes\bar N^{[1]}$; the Casimir acts on $\overline{\Psi_r(N)}$ with the single generalized eigenvalue $r^2$ (its eigenvalues on $\Psi_r(N)_\Q$ are $(p(b+1)\pm r)^2\equiv r^2$), and the generalized $r^2$-eigenspace of $V\otimes T(p-2+r)$ is $T(p-1+r)$, because $(r-2)^2\not\equiv r^2$ and $\Delta(p-1+r)$ is not simple ($E^{(s)}F^{(r)}v=\binom{p-1+s}{s}F^{(r-s)}v\equiv0$ for $1\le s\le r$, by Lucas). Dimensions agree.
(c) Path counting: for $k<p(\mu+1)$, $F^{(k)}(A_0n_0)$ is a unit times $A_{k\bmod p}n_{\lfloor k/p\rfloor}$ plus $C$-terms, and for $k=p(\mu+1)+t$, $0\le t\le r-1$, it is a unit times $C_{p-r+t}n_\mu$ (every branch through the coupling $pC_{p-r}$ contributes $p^{\mu+1}u^\mu\mu!$, and there are $\mu+1$ of them). So $\Dist\cdot A_0n_0\cong\Delta_A(p\mu+p-1+r)$ is saturated, and the quotient is spanned by the images of $F^{(k)}C_0n_0$, $k\le p\mu+p-1-r$.
\end{proof}

The characters are $\operatorname{ch}\Phi_p(N)=\chi_{p-1}\cdot(\operatorname{ch}N)(z^p)$ and $\operatorname{ch}\Psi_r(N)=(z^r+z^{-r})\chi_{p-1}\cdot(\operatorname{ch}N)(z^p)$; since $\chi_{p-1}(z)\chi_b(z^p)=\chi_{pb+p-1}(z)$, every Weyl factor $\Delta(b)$ of $N$ gives $\Delta(pb+p-1)$, respectively $\Delta(pb+p-1\pm r)$.

\begin{lemma}[the dance lattices]\label{dance}
Define $L(\lambda)=\Delta_A(\lambda)$ if $\lambda+1<p$; otherwise write $\lambda+1=d_0+p(b+1)$ with $0\le d_0\le p-1$ and put $L(\lambda)=\Phi_p(L(b))$ if $d_0=0$, $L(\lambda)=\Psi_{d_0}(L(b))$ if $d_0\ne0$. Then:
(i) $L(\lambda)$ has rank $d_kp^k2^{|I|}$ and a $\Delta$-filtration with $2^{|I|}$ factors;
(ii) $L(\lambda)\otimes\F_p\cong T(\lambda)$;
(iii) $V^{\otimes m}\otimes\Z_p\cong\bigoplus_\lambda L(\lambda)^{n_\lambda(m)}$; in particular $L(\lambda)\cong T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ and $Y_p(\lambda,i,e)=\coker\big(F+p^e(D+i)\text{ on }L(\lambda)\big)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) follows from Propositions~\ref{propPhi}(c) and~\ref{propPsi}(c) and exactness. (ii) By Propositions~\ref{propPhi}(b) and~\ref{propPsi}(b), $\overline{L(\lambda)}\cong T(p-1+d_0)\otimes\overline{L(b)}^{[1]}$ (with $T(p-1)=St$), which is $T(\lambda)$ by Donkin's tensor product theorem $T(p-1+r+pb)\cong T(p-1+r)\otimes T(b)^{[1]}$, $0\le r\le p-1$ \cite[Prop.~2.1]{Don}; for $SL_2$ in every characteristic $p$ see \cite[Prop.~3.4(a)]{STWZ} and \cite[Remark~1.1(ii)]{And}. (iii) The lattice $\bigoplus L(\lambda)^{n_\lambda(m)}$ has a $\Delta$-filtration, $V^{\otimes m}\otimes\Z_p$ has a $\nabla$-filtration, and their reductions are isomorphic by (ii) and the decomposition of the tilting module $V^{\otimes m}\otimes\F_p$ \cite[E.6]{Jan}. Since $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\Delta_A,\nabla_A)=0$, an isomorphism of the reductions lifts to a homomorphism over $\Z_p$, whose determinant is a unit. The existence and uniqueness of $T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ and the lifting of decompositions are classical \cite[\S1.2]{And}, \cite[II.E.19--E.22]{Jan}; what is new here is the explicit form of the lattices. The last statement is \eqref{eq:blocks}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
In the Lean proof (\S\ref{sec:lean}), the decomposition $V^{\otimes m}\otimes\Z_p\cong\bigoplus_\lambda L(\lambda)^{n_\lambda(m)}$ of (iii), with $n_\lambda(m)$ given by the recursion of the tensor rules, is proved without Donkin's theorem and without the lifting lemma: for $1\le r\le p-1$ there are explicit short exact sequences $0\to\Psi_{r+1}(N)\to V\otimes\Psi_r(N)\to Q_{r-1}(N)\to0$ (with $\Psi_p(N):=\Phi_p(V\otimes N)$, $Q_{r-1}=\Psi_{r-1}$ for $r\ge2$, $Q_0=\Phi_p(N)^2$), and they split over $\Z_p$ by a Fitting idempotent of the Casimir, whose two eigenvalue classes $(r+1)^2$ and $(r-1)^2$ differ modulo the odd prime $p$. Induction on $m$ then gives it. The identification $L(\lambda)\cong T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ is not formalized.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Payment systems and the moves}
Let $N$ be a lattice graded by floors. A \emph{payment system} of size $R$ on $N^{\oplus R}$ is the operator $G(x\iota_{s'})=Fx\,\iota_{s'}+\sum_sP(\varphi)_{ss'}\,x\iota_s$ for $x$ on floor $\varphi$, with $P(\varphi)\in M_R(A)$ lower triangular; it is \emph{even} if every $P(\varphi)\in pM_R(A)$. Two systems are equivalent ($\sim$) when they have the same Smith form. The operator $F+p^e(D+i)$ on $L(\lambda)$ is an even system of size $1$ with $P_0(\varphi)=p^e(\varphi+i)$, since $e\ge1$.

\begin{proposition}[twist move]\label{twistmove}
Let $N=\Phi_p(N')$ and let $G$ be even of size $R$. Then $G\sim I_{(p-1)R\operatorname{rank}N'}\oplus pG'$, with $G'$ even of size $R$ on $N'$ and
$P'(f)=(-1)^{p-1}u^{-1}p^{-1}P(pf)P(pf+1)\cdots P(pf+p-1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $j\le p-2$ the relation of $b_jn\iota_{s'}$ has coefficient $1$ on $b_{j+1}n\iota_{s'}$ and its other terms on $b_j$; eliminating these unit pivots gives, for the rows of generators $g_j(n)=(b_jn\iota_1,\dots,b_jn\iota_R)$, $g_{j+1}(n)=-g_j(n)P(pf+j)$. The remaining relation, of $b_{p-1}n$, is $pu\,g_0(Fn)+g_{p-1}(n)P(pf+p-1)=0$, that is $pu\,[g_0(Fn)+g_0(n)P'(f)]=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[split move]\label{splitmove}
Let $N=\Psi_r(N')$ and let $G$ be even of size $R$. Then $G\sim I_{2(p-1)R\operatorname{rank}N'}\oplus pG''$, with $G''$ even of size $2R$ on $N'$ (slots $(s,A)$, then $(s,C)$) and
\[
P''(f)=\begin{pmatrix}Q_A(f)&0\\ K(f)&Q_C(f)\end{pmatrix},\quad
\begin{aligned}Q_A(f)&=(-1)^{p-1}u^{-1}p^{-1}P(pf)\cdots P(pf+p-1),\\ Q_C(f)&=(-1)^{p-1}u^{-1}p^{-1}P(pf+r)\cdots P(pf+r+p-1),\\ K(f)&=(-1)^{p-r}u^{-1}P(pf+r)\cdots P(pf+p-1).\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As for the twist: eliminate the $2(p-1)$ unit pivots per $n$ and slot; the relation of $A_{p-1}n$, from $F(A_{p-1}n)=puA_0(Fn)+pC_{p-r}n$, becomes $pu[a_0(Fn)+a_0(n)Q_A(f)+c_0(n)K(f)]=0$, and that of $C_{p-1}n$ becomes $pu[c_0(Fn)+c_0(n)Q_C(f)]=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[last level]\label{lastlevel}
For $G$ even of size $R$ on $\Delta_A(d-1)^{\oplus R}$, $1\le d\le p-1$: $G\sim I_{(d-1)R}\oplus M$ with $M=(-1)^{d-1}((d-1)!)^{-1}P(0)P(1)\cdots P(d-1)\in p^dM_R(A)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The pivots $s+1\le d-1\le p-2$ are units: $g_{s+1}=-g_sP(s)/(s+1)$, and the last relation is $g_{d-1}P(d-1)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{thmDp}
Let $\lambda\ge0$, $e\ge1$, $i\ge0$. Put $P_0(\varphi)=p^e(\varphi+i)$ and, for $t=0,\dots,k-1$, let $P_{t+1}$ be the twist (Proposition~\ref{twistmove}) of $P_t$ if $d_t=0$ and the split with $r=d_t$ (Proposition~\ref{splitmove}) if $d_t\ne0$. Let $M_p(\lambda,i,e)=P_k(0)P_k(1)\cdots P_k(d_k-1)$, a lower triangular matrix of size $2^{|I|}$ with entries in $p^{d_k}\Z_p$. Then
\[
Y_p(\lambda,i,e)\;\cong\;S_p(\lambda)\oplus\coker\big(p^kM_p(\lambda,i,e)\big).
\]
\end{thmDp}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{dance}, $Y_p(\lambda,i,e)$ is the cokernel of an even system of size $1$ on $L(\lambda)$. Apply the move of digit $t$ for $t=0,1,\dots,k-1$: before move $t$ the lattice is $L(\lambda_t)$, $\lambda_t+1=\sum_{t'\ge t}d_{t'}p^{t'-t}$, carrying $2^{|I\cap[0,t)|}$ slots, and the move eliminates $(p-1)d_kp^{k-t-1}2^{|I|}$ unit pivots and multiplies the rest by $p$. After $k$ moves the system lives on $\Delta_A(d_k-1)$ with $2^{|I|}$ slots, and Proposition~\ref{lastlevel} applies. So the operator is equivalent to $I_{n_0}\oplus p(I_{n_1}\oplus p(\cdots\oplus p(I_{(d_k-1)2^{|I|}}\oplus M)\cdots))$ with $n_t=(p-1)d_kp^{k-t-1}2^{|I|}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thI}]
For odd $p$, by Theorem D$_p$, $Y_p(\lambda,i,e)\cong S_p(\lambda)\oplus\coker(p^kM)$ with $M$ of size $2^{|I|}$ and all entries in $p\Z_p$. Every Smith exponent of $M$ is then $\ge1$ or $\infty$, so $\coker(p^kM)$ is the sum of exactly $2^{|I|}$ cyclic modules, each $\Z_p$ or of order $\ge p^{k+1}$, while every summand of $S_p(\lambda)$ has order $\le p^k$. By the uniqueness of elementary divisors the statement follows. For $p=2$, \cite[Theorem~D]{DST} gives $Y_2(\lambda,i,e)=X^{(e)}(\lambda,i)\cong\bigoplus_{a=1}^{k-1}(\Z/2^a)^{2^{|I|+k-1-a}}\oplus\coker(2^kM)$ with $M$ the final matrix of an even payment system \cite[Proposition~4.3]{DST}, of size $2^{|I|}$ with even entries; the same argument applies, and $S_2(\lambda)$ is the displayed group with $d_k=1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The entries of $M_p$}
Let $\mathcal{C}=\Q[x_t:t\in I]/(x_t^2)$. Define $c^{(0)}=1$ and $c^{(h+1)}=a\,(c^{(h)})^p+[h\in I]\,b_h\,x_h\,(c^{(h)})^{p-d_h}$, with $a=(-1)^{p-1}/(up)$ and $b_h=(-1)^{p-d_h}/u$. For $\Delta\subseteq I$ write $[\,\cdot\,]_\Delta$ for the coefficient of $x^\Delta=\prod_{t\in\Delta}x_t$.

\begin{lemma}[windows]\label{windows}
After $h$ moves the slots are the $D\subseteq I\cap[0,h)$, and $P_h(f)_{DD'}=[c^{(h)}]_{D\setminus D'}\prod_{d\in W}p^e(i+d)$ for $D'\subseteq D$ (and $0$ otherwise), with $W=[p^hf+o_D,\ p^hf+o_{D'}+p^h-1]$. Consequently
\[
M_p(\lambda,i,e)_{DD'}=\frac{(-1)^{d_k-1}}{(d_k-1)!}\,\big[(c^{(k)})^{d_k}\big]_{D\setminus D'}\prod_{d=o_D}^{o_{D'}+K-1}p^e(i+d).
\]
With $c^{(h)}=a^{(p^h-1)/(p-1)}q_h(y)$ and $y_t=-b_t(-p)^{1+d_t(p^t-1)/(p-1)}x_t$, the polynomials $q_h$ satisfy $q_0=1$, $q_{t+1}=q_t^p-[t\in I]\,y_t\,q_t^{p-d_t}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
As \cite[Theorem~4.4]{DST}: an entry of a product $P_h(\varphi_1)\cdots P_h(\varphi_s)$ over consecutive floors is a sum over chains $D=T_0\supseteq\cdots\supseteq T_s=D'$; the windows of consecutive factors are adjacent, so every chain gives the same product of payments, and the coefficients add up to $[(c^{(h)})^s]_{D\setminus D'}$. The twist uses $s=p$ from floor $pf$; the split uses $s=p$ from $pf$ ($Q_A$), $s=p$ from $pf+r$ ($Q_C$, slot $D\cup\{h\}$, $o_{D\cup\{h\}}=o_D+d_hp^h$) and $s=p-r$ from $pf+r$ ($K$); the last level uses $s=d_k$ from floor $0$. The normalization is a substitution.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[deficit law]\label{deficit}
$[q_k^{d_k}]_\emptyset=1$, and $v_p\big([q_k^{d_k}]_\Delta\big)=k-|\Delta|-\min\Delta$ for $\emptyset\ne\Delta\subseteq I$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induction on $|\Delta|$ and on $t>\max\Delta$, as in \cite[Lemma~4.5]{DST}. With $m=\max\Delta$, $[q_{m+1}]_\Delta=-[q_m^{p-d_m}]_{\Delta\setminus\{m\}}$. For a power $N$ of $q$, $[q^N]_\Gamma$ is the sum over the set partitions of $\Gamma$ into $j\le N$ blocks of $N(N-1)\cdots(N-j+1)\prod_B[q]_B$. For $N=p-d_m\in[1,p-1]$ the single-block term has a unit factor; for $N=p$ (every later move) every falling factorial $(p)_j$, $1\le j\le p$, has valuation $1$. In both cases a partition into $j\ge2$ blocks has valuation larger than the single-block one, by induction. So the valuation is $m-(|\Delta|-1)-\min\Delta$ at $t=m+1$ and grows by exactly $1$ at each later move; the final power $d_k$ is a unit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[cost form]\label{costform}
Let $M=M_p(\lambda,i,e)$ and $b\subseteq a\subseteq I$, and let $\gamma(\Delta)=\sum_{u\in I\setminus\Delta,\ u>\min\Delta}h_u$ ($\gamma(\emptyset)=0$). If $i\ge1$, then $v_p(M_{ab})=eK+\kappa_p(m,K)+R_m(a)-R_{m+K}(b)+\gamma(a\setminus b)$ with $m=i-1$. If $i=0$, the row $\emptyset$ is zero and, for $a\ne\emptyset$, $v_p(M_{ab})=eK+R^\sharp(a)-R^\sharp(b)+\gamma(a\setminus b)$. All entries with $b\subseteq a$ ($a\ne\emptyset$ when $i=0$) are nonzero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemmas~\ref{windows} and~\ref{deficit}, $v_p(M_{ab})=-d_k\frac{p^k-1}{p-1}+\sum_{t\in\Delta}\big(1+d_t\frac{p^t-1}{p-1}\big)+\delta(\Delta)+e(K-o_\Delta)+\sum_{d=o_a}^{o_b+K-1}v_p(i+d)$, $\Delta=a\setminus b$, $\delta(\Delta)=k-|\Delta|-\min\Delta$. Legendre's formula $v_p(n!)=(n-s_p(n))/(p-1)$, the identity $s_p(x+o_E)=s_p(x)+\sum_{t\in E}d_t-(p-1)\kappa_p(x,o_E)$, $s_p(K)=d_k$, and $|\Delta|+\delta(\Delta)=\sum_{t\in a}h_t-\sum_{t\in b}h_t+\gamma(\Delta)$ \cite[Lemma~5.1]{DST} collapse this to the stated form. For $i=0$ use also $\kappa_p(K-1,o_E)=k-\min E$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Smith form of $M_p$}

\begin{lemma}\label{integral}
The real antitonic fits of the clocks $x$ and $x^\sharp$ of \S\ref{sec:conjI} take integer values.
\end{lemma}

\begin{thmSmith}
For $i\ge1$ the Smith exponents of $M_p(\lambda,i,e)$ are $eK+\kappa_p(i-1,K)+\hat y_D$, $D\subseteq I$; for $i=0$ they are $\infty$ (once) and $eK+\hat y^\sharp_D$, $\emptyset\ne D\subseteq I$.
\end{thmSmith}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the Smith form and of Lemma~\ref{integral}]
The proof of the case $p=2$, \cite[\S6--\S7]{DST}, goes through for odd $p$ with the changes listed below; every step not listed is used verbatim. A complete line-by-line version for odd $p$, written by the independent reader, is part of the record of this paper (file \texttt{THEOREM\_F\_ODD\_P.md}).

\emph{(O1) The order.} Since $d_tp^t>\sum_{u<t,\,u\in I}d_up^u$, the o-order is the binary order of the digit sets, as for $p=2$; in particular all dancers without $c=\max I$ precede all dancers with $c$, and the two halves have $2^{|I|-1}$ elements each.

\emph{Ceiling} \cite[\S6]{DST}. The tropical bound (Lemma~6.1), the unique golden matching (Theorem~6.2), which is a statement about the binary numbers of (O1) and the quantity $\gamma$ only, the natural minor (Corollary~6.3), which needs the cost form (Proposition~\ref{costform}) and the non-vanishing of the entries, convexity (Lemma~6.4) and Theorem~6.5 hold verbatim.

\emph{Floor} \cite[\S7]{DST}. Lemmas~7.1--7.3 and Theorem~7.4 are statements about sequences and hold verbatim. A \emph{house} is $H=(I,k,\ell,e,(d_t))$ with $0\le\ell<p^k$, with rulers $R_H(E)=\sum_{t\in E}(h_t-ed_tp^t)-\kappa_p(\ell,o_E)$ and $J_H(E)=[\ell+o_E\ge p^k]$, an up-set.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Lemma 7.5$_p$.} With $\ell=m\bmod p^k$ and $r_k(\mu)$ the number of consecutive digits $p-1$ of $\mu$ from digit $k$ on, $R_m=R_H-r_k(m)J_H$ and $R_{m+K}=R_H-r_k(m+K)J_H$.
\item \emph{Lemma 7.6$_p$ (gates).} In the window $W_t=[t,\mathrm{next}(t))$ the addend has the single digit $[t\in E]\,d_t$ at $t$; put $\theta_t=p-\ell_t\in[1,p]$, $\kappa_t=1+\min(r_{t+1}(\ell),h_t-1)$ and $\mathrm{sat}_t=[\kappa_t=h_t]$. The window makes $\kappa_t$ carries if and only if $[t\in E]d_t+c_t\ge\theta_t$ ($c_t$ the incoming carry), so it has one of four types: OR ($\theta_t=1$), DIG ($2\le\theta_t\le d_t$, carries iff $t\in E$), AND ($\theta_t=d_t+1$) and NEV ($\theta_t\ge d_t+2$, never). A carry leaves the window if and only if it carries and is saturated. For $p=2$ only OR and AND occur.
\item \emph{Lemma 7.7$_p$.} For $c=\max I$, $H'=(I\setminus\{c\},c,\ell\bmod p^c,e)$, $h=k-c$ and $\tau=h-ed_cp^c-[\mathrm{OR}\vee\mathrm{DIG}]\kappa$:
$R_H(D_0)=R_{H'}(D_0)-[\mathrm{OR}]\kappa J'(D_0)$, $R_H(D_0\cup\{c\})=\tau+R_{H'}(D_0)-[\mathrm{AND}]\kappa J'(D_0)$, $J_H(D_0)=[\mathrm{OR}]\,\mathrm{sat}\,J'(D_0)$, $J_H(D_0\cup\{c\})=\mathrm{sat}\,([\mathrm{OR}\vee\mathrm{DIG}]+[\mathrm{AND}]J'(D_0))$.
\item \emph{Theorem 7.8$_p$} (the induction of \cite[Theorem~7.8]{DST}, steps 1--8) with the bound $(\Lambda)$ replaced by $(\Lambda_p)$: $|\hat y_H|\le e\,o_I$. Step 2 uses $d_1\le ed_cp^c-h+\kappa\le ed_cp^c$ and $o_{I'}+d_cp^c=o_I$; in steps 3(a), 3(b) and 5 the quantity $\alpha2^c-\alpha(2^c-1)=\alpha$ becomes $e(d_cp^c-o_{I'})\ge e$, and $h-\alpha$ becomes $h-e$; in 3(c), $X_2(b')=\hat y'(b')-ed_cp^c\le-e$. The two new window types fall into existing cases: DIG with $\mathrm{sat}=1$ has $J_H=(0,1)$ and constant shift $d_1=ed_cp^c$, which is case 3(b); DIG with $\mathrm{sat}=0$, and NEV, have $J_H\equiv0$, which is case 3(d). Steps 4, 6, 7 and 8 hold with $\alpha$ replaced by $e$, with the inherited potential $v'(D_0)=V'(D_0)-[\mathrm{OR}]\kappa J'(D_0)$, $v'(D_0\cup\{c\})=\tau+V'(D_0)-[\mathrm{AND}]\kappa J'(D_0)$.
\item Lemma~7.9 (integrality of the real fits, which is Lemma~\ref{integral}) and Theorem~7.10 follow verbatim, with the penalty cells $\mathrm{Cell}(H;r_k(m),r_k(m+K))$; for $i=0$ the house is $\ell=p^k-1$, all windows are of type OR, and $J=[D\ne\emptyset]$.
\end{itemize}
Every claim of this list was also checked numerically, in $56\,246$ houses by the constructor and in $47\,310$ by the independent reader (\S\ref{sec:ver}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thJ}]
Combine Theorem D$_p$ with the Smith form of $M_p$: $\coker(p^kM_p)$ has the Smith exponents of $M_p$ raised by $k$. The statement on $K(H(d,q))$ follows from Theorem~\ref{thA} and \eqref{eq:blocks}, with Lemma~\ref{dance}(iii) for the multiplicities; for $p=2$ it is Theorem~\ref{thP}.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification}\label{sec:ver}

All computations were exact. The Smith forms over $\Z_{(p)}$ were computed modulo $p^M$ with $p^M<2^{31}$, by unit pivots and Schur complements, level by level in the $p$-adic valuation; factors of valuation at least $M$ were then determined separately, by the matrix $p^E M^{-1}$ of Lemma~\ref{inverse}, whose elementary divisors are $p^{E-a}$ for the divisors $p^a$ of $M$. The scripts, the sealed prediction files and the logs are at \url{https://github.com/tretoef-estrella/coloured-sand-theorem} and in the Zenodo record \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23327608}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.23327608} (all versions).

\begin{enumerate}

\section{Formal verification in Lean}\label{sec:lean}

The proofs are checked in the proof assistant Lean~4 with the Mathlib library. The text was cut into short self-contained pieces; Aristotle, an AI system made by Harmonic \cite{Har}, wrote the Lean proofs; on the author's machine each returned project was compared with the previous one, searched for forbidden constructs (\texttt{sorry}, new axioms, \texttt{native\_decide}), compiled module by module, and its main statements were restated by hand in a check file whose axioms were printed. Every certified piece uses only the standard axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

\textbf{Version~3 is checked.} Theorems A--H, Lemmas~2--5, \ref{forced} and~\ref{blocks}, Corollaries~\ref{corq} and~\ref{corN}, and the Example of Section~6 are proved in Lean, in the generality of version~3 (Theorem~\ref{thBprime} for $p\mid q$, Theorems~\ref{glue} and~\ref{thG} for odd $p$; the other cases are checked by the pieces of the next paragraph), with nothing cited: the tilting lattices of Section~6, their tensor rules and the decomposition of $V^{\otimes m}\otimes\Z_p$ for $m\le p^2+p-1$ are proved from scratch, as is Wilson's rank formula in the range used by Theorem~\ref{thD}; Theorem~\ref{thP} uses the Lean proof of \cite{DST}. At that stage the pieces of version~3 had $110$ files, $20\,765$ lines and $1\,032$ lines beginning with \texttt{theorem} or \texttt{lemma}, with no \texttt{sorry}.
\textbf{Theorems I and J are checked.} Their proof was cut into $19$ pieces, all certified: the twist $\Phi_p$ (Definition~\ref{defPhi}, Proposition~\ref{propPhi}) and its reduction modulo $p$; the split $\Psi_r$; the Casimir and the short exact sequences of the Remark after Lemma~\ref{dance}, and their splitting over $\Z_{(p)}$; the dance lattices $L(\lambda)$ and $V^{\otimes m}\otimes\Z_p\cong\bigoplus L(\lambda)^{n_\lambda(m)}$ for every $m$, without Donkin's theorem; the twist move (Proposition~\ref{twistmove}), the split move (Proposition~\ref{splitmove}) and the last level; Theorem~D$_p$ and Theorem~\ref{thI}; the deficit law, the windows, the cost form, the ceiling, the houses, Theorem~F at $p$, the integrality and the floor of every cell; the Smith form of the final matrix; and Theorem~\ref{thJ}. The statement checked for Theorem~\ref{thJ} is about the sandpile group itself: the torsion of the cokernel of Mathlib's Laplacian matrix of the Hamming graph $H(d,q)$, for every odd prime $p$, every $q\ge2$ with $p\mid q$ and every $d\ge0$. The multiplicities $n_\lambda(m)$ enter through the recursion given by the tensor rules, which is proved to describe $V^{\otimes m}\otimes\Z_p$; the description by characters at the end of Theorem~\ref{thJ} is a way to compute them and is not formalized.

\textbf{Every prime.} Three further pieces check the cases that the pieces above left out: Theorem~\ref{thBprime} for $p\nmid q$ (the pieces above assume $p\mid q$); Theorem~\ref{thI} for $p=2$, stated on the groups $X^{(e)}(\lambda,i)$ of \cite{DST}, which on paper are the blocks $Y_2(\lambda,i,e)$ of Theorem~\ref{thP}; and Theorems~\ref{glue} and~\ref{thG} for $p=2$, with the bounds $m\le2$ and $m\le5$. With them, every result of the paper is checked for every prime. In Lean, Theorem~\ref{thI} and the block formulas of Theorems~\ref{thJ} and~\ref{thP} are proved for explicit dance lattices: $L(\lambda)$ for odd $p$, and the lattices of \cite{DST} for $p=2$; their identification with $T_{\Z_p}(\lambda)$ (Lemma~\ref{dance}(ii); \cite[Remark~(1) after Theorem~3.6]{DST}) is not formalized and is not needed for the statements on $K(H(d,q))$. Theorem~\ref{thBprime} is stated in Lean for the $\Z_p$-module $\operatorname{coker}(q(c+H)+U)$ on $B_m(\Z_p)$, free summand included; its form for $\Syl_pX_q(m,c)$ follows in a few lines and is checked too (\texttt{checks/CheckTheoremEPaperForm.lean}, written by the fifth reader).

All $36$ pieces live in one Lean project, which also holds the Lean proof of \cite{DST}. Two groups of pieces defined the same names; the names were changed on one side only: in the three newer pieces of the first clash and in the files that use them ($14$ files in five pieces), and, for the second, in the piece that no other module imports (five files in one piece), and both renames were checked to be pure (undoing them gives back the original files byte for byte). The final check is six files; each loads all $36$ pieces together and repeats the hand restatements of a part of them. They compile with no error, and each of their $451$ \texttt{\#print axioms} commands answers with the standard axioms only. The pieces of this paper have $254$ files, $46\,787$ lines and $2\,370$ lines beginning with \texttt{theorem} or \texttt{lemma}, with no \texttt{sorry}. The whole Lean project was rebuilt from its sources, on Mathlib's published build, with the final check, in GitHub Actions run~38724562833 of the repository \url{https://github.com/tretoef-estrella/coloured-sand-theorem}, at commit \texttt{51c25f0}, on 15 October 2026. The Lean project is in the folder \texttt{lean/} of that repository; the Lean certificate, with the full statements, the dependency cone of the main theorems and the data of every piece, is deposited with this version in its Zenodo record.
